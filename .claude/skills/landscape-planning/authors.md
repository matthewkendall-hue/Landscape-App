# Foundational Texts & Authors

## Ian McHarg — Design with Nature (1969)

The most influential ecological planning text of the 20th century. McHarg pioneered the idea that human settlement must be designed with natural processes, not against them.

Core Contributions:
- The Overlay / Layer-Cake Method — Layered transparent maps of natural features (geology, hydrology, soils, vegetation, wildlife, climate) to reveal land-use suitability. Direct precursor to modern GIS.
- Suitability Analysis — Systematic framework for determining ecologically appropriate land uses based on composite overlay of environmental constraints and opportunities.
- Multidisciplinary Integration — Planning must synthesize geology, hydrology, soil science, botany, zoology, and climatology holistically.
- Critique of Conventional Development — Sprawling, nature-blind development causes flooding, erosion, pollution, habitat destruction — all avoidable through ecological planning.

Key Case Studies:
- Staten Island — Regional ecological inventory and suitability mapping
- The Valleys (Baltimore County) — Development following natural drainage and woodland patterns
- Plan for the Potomac — Watershed-scale ecological planning

Legacy: Established landscape architects as environmental stewards. Methods underpin modern EIAs, green infrastructure planning, and GIS-based land-use analysis worldwide.

## John Ormsbee Simonds — Landscape Architecture: A Manual of Site Planning and Design (1961)

The foremost practical reference for 50+ years. Systematic, step-by-step procedures from site analysis through detailed design.

Core Contributions:
- Site Analysis to Programming to Schematic Design to Detail Design — Clear procedural framework.
- Spatial Design with Hand Sketches — Hundreds of diagrams illustrating enclosure, movement, proportion, human-scale design.
- Topography and Grading — Detailed treatment of contours, cut-and-fill, drainage design.
- Sustainability and Growth Management — Later editions: urban sprawl reversal, open-space planning, regional greenways/blueways.
- Human Experience — How people perceive, move through, and emotionally respond to outdoor spaces.

## Frederick Law Olmsted — Father of American Landscape Architecture

Six enduring design principles:
1. Genius of Place — Design responds to unique site characteristics, including disadvantages
2. Unified Composition — Every element serves an overarching design purpose; no gratuitous decoration
3. Orchestration of Movement — Subtly guide circulation; separate traffic types
4. Orchestration of Use — Distribute activities into zones to prevent conflicts
5. Sustainable Design and Conservation — Plants that thrive with minimal maintenance; conserve natural features
6. Comprehensive Approach — Landscapes extend benefits beyond boundaries through connected greenways

Landmark Projects: Central Park (NYC), Prospect Park (Brooklyn), The Emerald Necklace (Boston), Biltmore Estate (Asheville).

## Garrett Eckbo — Landscape for Living (1950)

Defined Modernist landscape architecture. Rejected formal-vs-informal debate: "Why not biology plus geometry?"

Core Contributions:
- Landscape as Social Space — Landscape is the interaction of people and place; exterior spatial design is the purposeful formation of that interaction.
- Rejection of Pure Naturalism — Criticized Olmsted-era "palliative" park design; advocated scientific method free of preconceptions.
- Mass, Void, Balance and Line — Prioritized spatial composition over decorative rules.
- Multidisciplinary Modernism — Influenced by Walter Gropius and the Bauhaus; landscape design as social change.
- Materials-First Thinking — Earth, rock, water, plants, and structural elements as the designer's palette.

## Anne Whiston Spirn — The Granite Garden: Urban Nature and Human Design (1984)

Named one of 100 most important books of the 20th century (APA). Launched the ecological urbanism movement.

Core Contributions:
- Nature Is Already in the City — Cities have their own air, water, geology, plant, and animal systems.
- Urban Ecology as Design Framework — Integrated air quality, water management, urban soils, geology, urban wildlife habitat.
- Process over Form — Designers who ignore processes that shape landscapes during and after construction produce designs that fail.

## Geoffrey and Susan Jellicoe — The Landscape of Man (1975)

Sweeping historical survey from ancient civilizations to modern era, tracing how cultures shaped land to reflect values, beliefs, and social structures.

## Other Essential Figures

- Kevin Lynch — The Image of the City (1960): How people perceive urban space: paths, edges, districts, nodes, landmarks
- Lawrence Halprin — The RSVP Cycles (1969): Participatory design process; movement-based design (Freeway Park, FDR Memorial)
- Sylvia Crowe — Garden Design (1958): English landscape tradition; garden design integrated with broader land use
- Richard Forman — Landscape Ecology (1986): Patch-Corridor-Matrix model for understanding land mosaics
