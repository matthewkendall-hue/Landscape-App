# Landscape Ecology

## Forman's Patch-Corridor-Matrix Model

Richard Forman's 1986 model describes three fundamental elements of every landscape:

Patch — A relatively homogeneous area differing from its surroundings.
Examples: forest fragment, wetland, meadow, park.

Corridor — A narrow strip connecting patches.
Examples: stream buffer, hedgerow, greenway, road verge.

Matrix — The dominant, most connected landscape element that plays the dominant role in landscape functioning.
Examples: agricultural land, suburban development, forest.

## Planning Principles from Landscape Ecology

1. Preserve large patches — Large, contiguous natural areas support more species and ecological processes than the same area fragmented into small patches
2. Maintain corridors — Connect patches to allow species movement and genetic exchange
3. Minimize road impacts — Roads fragment habitat and cause wildlife mortality; minimize roads in and near large natural areas
4. Buffer sensitive areas — Provide transition zones between development and natural areas
5. Work with the matrix — Even dominant developed landscape can be made more permeable to wildlife through native plantings, green corridors, and reduced barriers
